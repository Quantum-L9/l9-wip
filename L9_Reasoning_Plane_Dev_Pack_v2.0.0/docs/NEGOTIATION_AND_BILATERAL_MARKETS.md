# Negotiation and Bilateral Market Reasoning

Brokerage is naturally bilateral: both acquisition price and disposition price may be strategic outcomes rather than fixed inputs.

## Two uncertain reservation surfaces

```text
SUPPLIER
minimum acceptable price (often hidden)
          ↓
        BROKER
          ↑
BUYER
maximum willingness to pay (often hidden)
```

Supplier ask and buyer stated maximum are observations, not necessarily reservation values.

Beliefs may use:
- past transactions with the actor;
- comparable-market transactions;
- supply/demand state;
- current inventory/urgency signals;
- outside options;
- freight and execution economics;
- source ask and historical concession patterns;
- relationship history;
- credibility and information-quality signals.

## Price as strategy

Candidate pricing actions can be evaluated as response distributions, not deterministic outcomes. Example:

```text
quote .40 -> high acceptance probability, lower margin
quote .45 -> lower acceptance probability, higher margin
quote .50 -> materially higher walk risk, highest conditional margin
```

The buy side is symmetrical: lower bids may create larger spread but increase supplier walk/shopping risk. The optimal strategy may couple both sides of the transaction.

## Credibility

Credible material knowledge, complete specifications, logistics clarity, execution history, and consistent behavior can change counterparties' beliefs and response probabilities. Credibility is therefore economically relevant strategic state, but must be evidence-based rather than fabricated signaling.

## Repeated relationships

Repeated-game value includes reputation, trust, future transaction friction, relationship capital, consistency, and credibility. One-shot gain that damages future state may have lower Strategic Value than a smaller immediate margin with superior Systemic Future Value.
